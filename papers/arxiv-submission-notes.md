**Title** (unchanged): The Fundamental Theorem of Asset Pricing, Formalized in Lean 4

**Abstract** (1756 characters; arXiv limit 1920):

```
The Fundamental Theorem of Asset Pricing states that a market is free of arbitrage exactly when it admits an equivalent martingale measure. We formalize it in Lean 4 over Mathlib in three settings: a finite-state market with one risky asset over a finite horizon (Harrison–Pliska), and one-period markets with one asset or finitely many assets on an arbitrary probability space. The finite case is the geometry of a separating hyperplane. In the one-period case the proof builds the equivalent martingale measure from a minimiser of the potential $\theta\mapsto\mathbb{E}[\log(1+e^{\langle\theta,Y\rangle})]$: absence of arbitrage makes the potential coercive off the redundant directions, its first-order condition is the martingale property, and the logistic weight of the minimiser, normalised, is the density; a bounded tempering removes integrability, so the return need only be measurable. The construction uses no Hahn–Banach theorem, no $L^0$-closedness argument, no measurable selection and no non-redundancy hypothesis. One predicate serves every time index, with one forward theorem. The same separation also yields the Artzner–Delbaen–Eber–Heath representation of coherent risk measures on a finite state space. We add the second theorem on finite spaces (complete if and only if the martingale measure is unique) and, on any probability space, the reduction of the Dalang–Morton–Willinger theorem to its conditional one-period form. That conditional step, which needs a measurable selection, is assumed, not proved, so the multi-period theorem remains open; in continuous time only the forward direction, for simple strategies, is proved. Every theorem discussed is sorry-free and uses only Lean's three standard axioms, enforced by the build.
```

## arXiv:2606.15089 → v3

**Upload:** `dist/ito-arxiv.tar.gz` (`ito.tex` + `mathfin-paper.sty`; pdflatex).

**Title** (unchanged): A Machine-Checked Itô Calculus for Brownian Motion

**Abstract** (1907 characters; arXiv limit 1920):

```
We develop the Itô calculus of Brownian motion, machine-checked in Lean 4 over Mathlib and the BrownianMotion package. The Itô integral is built on the half-line as a Hilbert-space isometry, from a predictable-rectangle $\pi$-system through the density of simple adapted processes; the integral on $[0,T]$ is that map composed with extension by zero. One identity, that the integral at time $t$ is the conditional-expectation projection of its terminal value onto $\mathcal{F}_t$, makes it an $L^2$ martingale with its time-indexed isometry; a maximal inequality gives it a continuous modification, a local martingale. Its range together with the constants is all of $L^2(\mathcal{F}^B_T)$, which is martingale representation, proved by totality of Doléans exponentials without Malliavin calculus. Itô's formula for $f(t,B_t)$ is proved in $L^2$ for derivatives of at most exponential growth, with the integrand named; with no growth condition, the compensated process agrees at each time with a continuous local martingale. For Itô processes with continuous adapted coefficients and no bound it holds as an identity of processes, almost surely for all $t\le T$ at once; the product rule follows. Also proved: Lévy's characterisation, with $M^2-t$ a local martingale as hypothesis; Girsanov's theorem for bounded predictable drift, concluding a Brownian motion for the filtration when its paths are almost surely continuous; Lipschitz SDEs on every horizon, as an $L^2$ class; stochastic Fubini. To our knowledge these are the first machine-checked constructions of the Itô integral and proofs of Itô's formula in any proof assistant. The boundary is explicit: the integrator is Brownian motion or an Itô integral against it, and quadratic variation is established in mean, not pathwise. About 34,000 lines of Lean, sorry-free, with the axioms of each headline result pinned to Lean's three standard axioms.
```

## arXiv:2606.01356 → v4

**Title** (unchanged): A Formally Verified Library of Mathematical Finance in Lean 4

**Abstract** (1908 characters; arXiv limit 1920):

```
We describe a library of mathematical finance in the Lean 4 proof assistant, built on Mathlib and the BrownianMotion package. Its benchmark records 804 results from the literature, each with a Lean statement, a status, and a published account of what the formalization leaves out: 766 are proved in full, 18 restate an upstream theorem, and 20 are reduced cores that prove less than the result they are named after. The library is about 158,000 lines of Lean, contains no sorry, and a build-time audit pins every library constant that a benchmark proof cites to Lean's three standard axioms. The Itô integral is constructed rather than assumed. On it the library proves Itô's formula for $C^2$ functions of Itô processes as an identity of processes, Lévy's characterisation, martingale representation, Girsanov's theorem for bounded predictable drifts, which yields a Brownian motion for the filtration when the driver's paths are almost surely continuous, and unique $L^2$ solutions of Lipschitz stochastic differential equations on every horizon. These results reach the contracts a user prices. In a contract language that separates payoffs from models, the Black–Scholes call is replicated by its delta hedge up to every date before maturity, and every fixed-dated contract on the physical Black–Scholes asset has, under the Girsanov measure of a horizon containing its dates, its risk-neutral value. An audit of faithfulness makes the logical status of every claim checkable, and its review has caught what mechanical gates cannot see, including a convergence theorem whose hypothesis could never be satisfied. Nearly all of the mathematics is classical, and what a formal base over it produces is certified unification of known results; two contributed developments, by Robert Martin, address problems the library's own survey had listed as open. The contribution is methodological and infrastructural.
```
